When are wedding vendor payments due?

Your contract decides, not a rule of thumb. But the pattern is steady enough to plan around: a deposit when you book, sometimes installments, and a final balance that usually lands in the last month before the wedding. Here is what couples report, what it can look like on a calendar, and how to keep every date in one place.

Morrow & MadeUpdated Oct 24, 20268 minute read

The short version

  • Every vendor sets its own schedule in its contract. Find three dates: the deposit, any installments, and the final balance.
  • Couples in public wedding forums report deposits from about $200 up to as much as 50%, and final balances most often due two weeks to a month before the wedding. Anecdotal, not a survey.
  • Payments pile up near the end. In our fictional example, 40% of the scheduled money ($9,735 of $24,210) falls in the last eight weeks.
  • One row per payment, with six fields, is enough to see what is next, what is due soon and what is overdue.

Three kinds of payment

Deposit. Paid when you book. It reserves your date. Ask whether it is refundable and what happens to it if the date moves.

Installment. A payment in between, if the vendor splits the total over several dates. Not every vendor does.

Final balance. The rest of the contract price, due before the wedding or, for some vendors, after it. This is the payment that surprises people, because it is the largest and it arrives while you are busy.

Nobody publishes a standard payment schedule, because there is not one. What exists is couples describing their own contracts in public forums. We read those threads and kept the patterns that repeat.

Typical patterns reported by Abastio, Ivory Lane and The Knot. Your contract is what counts.

A payment plan with a vendor usually means paying in pieces by the original due date, not moving the due date to after the wedding. That is how a planner interviewed by The Knot put it, and it is worth asking about before you sign.

Two things in those threads are worth planning around. Caterers often want the final headcount at about the same time as the final payment, so a guest list that is still moving two weeks out costs you twice. And some photographers and videographers hold your files until the final payment clears, so that payment can fall after the wedding.

What to ask before you sign

  1. What is the deposit, and is it refundable?Get the amount and the cancellation terms in the contract. Ask what happens to the deposit if you reschedule.
  2. Are there installments, and on which dates?Put each date on your calendar the day you sign.
  3. When is the final balance due, and in what form?Ask the exact date, accepted payment methods and any card fee. Some vendors prefer checks.
  4. What else is due on the day?Ask about tips, overtime and meal counts so nothing is a surprise on the wedding day.
  5. When do they need the final headcount?Often close to the final payment. Put both dates side by side.

Track every payment

A plain table works. A paper list works. What does not work is keeping dates in email threads, because the second deposit is exactly the one that goes missing. Record these six fields for every payment and you can answer the three questions that matter: what is next, what is due soon, and what is overdue.

Common questions

How much is a deposit for a wedding venue?

It varies by venue. Couples in public wedding forums report everything from a flat amount of about $200 to hold a date up to as much as 50% of the total, with a common middle pattern of a percentage deposit followed by monthly payments. Your contract states the deposit and whether it is refundable.

When is the final wedding payment due?

It is set in each contract. Couples describe final balances anywhere from two weeks to a month before the wedding, caterers wanting a final headcount about 10 to 14 days out, and photographers or videographers sometimes holding files until the last payment. These are individual reports, so check your own contracts.

Do I have to pay every vendor on the same day?

No. Each vendor bills on its own schedule, which is why due dates scatter. Many couples list every payment in one place with its due date so the overlap is visible weeks ahead.

Can I pay a wedding vendor early?

Often, but not always. Some couples report discounts for paying in full at booking and some caterers that will not take payment before the event. Ask each vendor, and keep the confirmation for any early payment.

What should I record for each payment?

The vendor, whether it is a deposit, installment or final balance, the amount, the due date, the date you paid, who paid it and how. With those six fields you can see what is due next and what is overdue.
