Wedding budget breakdown by percentage, with dollar examples

A percentage breakdown is a good first draft for a wedding budget. It tells you roughly where the money goes before you have a single quote. Here are the 2026 averages, what they look like in dollars at four budget sizes, and where they stop working.

The short version

  • In The Knot's 2026 data, venue and rentals take about 29% of the average budget and catering, cake and drinks about 24%. Together, more than half.
  • Photo and video take about 10%, florals and decor about 9%, music and attire about 6% each.
  • The percentages describe the average $34,200 wedding. On a small budget, fixed costs like a photographer take a bigger share.

Why the percentages break on a small budget

The breakdown is a picture of the average wedding. Your budget is not the average, and some costs do not shrink when the total does. The Knot also publishes average prices for single vendors. Put them next to a $10,000 budget and the problem is easy to see.

Here is a quick check at $20,000 with 100 guests. The catering share is $4,800, or $48 a guest, against The Knot's $80 average per person before drinks, cake or service charges. One of three things has to give: a smaller guest list, a bigger catering share, or a different style of meal. Our cost per guest guide walks through that math with your own quotes.

On a small budget, the usual move is not to shrink every line by the same amount. It is to cut guests, which lowers the per-guest lines, or to drop whole categories, like a planner, transportation or extra entertainment, so the ones you keep can be done properly.

Move money to what you care about

The percentages say what couples spent on average, not what you should spend. If photos matter most to you, give photography 15% and take it from somewhere you care less about. A simple way to do it:

  1. Each of you picks your top three.Separately, then compare. The overlap gets protected.
  2. Pick two lines to keep small.Categories neither of you will remember, or that a friend or family member could cover.
  3. Move the money on paper.Raise the protected lines and lower the small ones until the total still matches your budget.
  4. Write the new shares down.They become your estimates. Every quote gets compared against them.

What the percentages leave out

A few costs hide outside the categories, and they are where budgets usually go over.

  • Service charges. The Knot says a venue or caterer service charge typically runs 20 to 25% of the food and drink total. On an $8,200 catering line, that is roughly another $1,600 to $2,000.
  • Tips. Many vendors are tipped on the day, and some contracts already include gratuity. Read the contract before you budget it twice.
  • Tax and delivery fees. Quotes do not always include them.
  • A buffer. The Knot suggests setting aside at least 5% of the budget for unforeseen costs. On $20,000, that is $1,000.

The simplest fix is to take the buffer off the top. Budget $19,000 of a $20,000 total across the categories and hold $1,000 back.

From percentages to real quotes

A percentage breakdown is only the first version of your budget. Once quotes arrive, each line should change from an estimate to the price you actually signed for, and later to what you have paid. Couples in planning threads describe that last step as the hard part: a deposit that never made it into the total, or two people each thinking the other had paid.

Common questions

What percentage of a wedding budget goes to the venue?

About 29% for the venue and rentals, according to The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study. Catering, cake and drinks take about 24%, so the two together are more than half of the average budget.

What percentage of a wedding budget should go to photography?

About 10% for photography and videography together in The Knot's 2026 data. The average photographer alone cost $3,000, which is 30% of a $10,000 budget, so on a small budget the share is usually higher.

How much should you keep for unexpected wedding costs?

The Knot suggests setting aside at least 5% of the budget for unforeseen costs. Service charges, which The Knot says typically run 20 to 25% of the food and drink total, and vendor tips are two common surprises.

Do wedding budget percentages work for a $10,000 wedding?

Only roughly. The percentages describe the average $34,200 wedding. Some costs, like a photographer or attire, do not shrink with the budget, so a smaller wedding usually spends a bigger share on them and saves by cutting guests or whole categories.
