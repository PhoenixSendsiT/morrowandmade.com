Wedding budget guide

How much does a 100-person wedding cost?

There is no single price, and anyone who quotes one without your venue and menu is guessing. What you can do is check your plan against two published national averages, then build your own number from two parts: costs that stay the same however many people come, and costs that grow with every guest.

Morrow & MadeUpdated Oct 24, 20268 minute read

The short version

  • The 2026 national averages are $34,200 for 117 guests (The Knot) and about $36,000 for 145 guests (Zola). That works out to roughly $292 and $248 a guest.
  • Dividing a total by guests is a quick check, not a price. It spreads fixed costs, like the photographer, across everyone.
  • Your formula: fixed costs + (guests × per-guest costs). In our fictional example, 10 more guests add about $1,000.
  • Get per-guest prices from your caterer in writing, with service charge and tax, before you lock a guest count.

What the national numbers say

Two big 2026 studies give the most-quoted figures. The Knot’s 2026 Real Weddings Study, a survey of couples married in 2025, reports an average cost of $34,200 and about 117 guests. Zola’s 2026 First Look report, a survey of couples on Zola, reports an average of $36,000 and about 145 guests. The two come from different groups of couples, so they should not be treated as one trend line.

A quick check for 100 guests

100 × $248 = $24,800 and 100 × $292 = $29,200. Treat that range only as a first reality check. It is our arithmetic on two averages, not a quote or a prediction. Your city, venue, menu and season decide the real number.

Why total ÷ guests misleads

An average per guest sounds like a price tag, but it is a division. Your photographer charges for hours, not heads, so a bigger guest list makes the photographer look cheaper per person without saving you a cent. The next guest does not cost the average. They cost whatever the caterer, the bar and the rental company charge for one more place setting.

Build your own formula

You only need two lists and one line of arithmetic.

  1. List what does not change with headcount.Photographer, music, officiant, attire, and any venue fee that is flat. These are your fixed costs.
  2. List what is priced per guest.Catering, drinks, dessert, chairs and linens, invitations. Write down the price for one guest.
  3. Ask for the prices in writing.A per-person price can leave out service charge and tax, so ask for the total per person.
  4. Add it up.Total = fixed costs + guests × per-guest costs. Try three headcounts to see the gap.

Cake is a good example

Bakeries often quote cake per serving. Ten more guests at $6 each add $60 to the cake line. It is a small number, which is the point: no single per-guest line is scary, but five or six of them together are what move your total.

Price your guest count before you sign

  1. Ask the venue for capacity and minimums.Some venues set a food and beverage minimum, which is a floor on spend however many people come.
  2. Ask the caterer for one all-in per-person price.Include service charge, tax and any staffing or rental fees, so the number you multiply is the number you pay.
  3. Ask when the final headcount is due.Couples in public wedding forums describe caterers wanting it roughly 10 to 14 days before the wedding. Your contract has the actual date. We cover the full payment timing in our guide to when vendor payments are due.
  4. Run the formula at three headcounts.For example 80, 100 and 120. The difference between the totals is what the next 20 guests really cost you.

Common questions

How much does a 100-person wedding cost?

There is no single price. As a rough reality check, dividing the two 2026 national averages by their guest counts gives about $248 and $292 a guest, so 100 guests would land near $24,800 to $29,200. That is our arithmetic, not a quote: your region, venue, menu and season can move it a lot. Build your own number as fixed costs plus guests times per-guest costs.

Does the cost per guest go down when the guest list grows?

The average per guest tends to fall, because fixed costs like the photographer and the venue fee are spread across more people. The total still rises, because every extra guest brings their own food, drinks and rentals. Ask what the next guest costs, not what the average guest costs.

Which wedding costs change with the guest count?

Usually food, drinks, cake or desserts, chairs, tables and linens, invitations and postage, and any service charge or tax calculated on food and drink. Costs that usually stay the same are the photographer, videographer, music, officiant and attire. Venue, flowers, staffing and tips depend on the contract, so ask.

Is $292 per guest what I should budget?

No. $292 is a total divided by guests. It mixes costs that do not change with headcount and costs that do, so it overstates what one more guest adds. Use it as a check on your own total, not as a price.

How do I find my own per-guest cost?

Ask your caterer for the per-person price in writing, including service charge and tax, then add the per-person drinks, dessert and rental prices. Our fictional example uses $70, $24 and $6 a guest.
