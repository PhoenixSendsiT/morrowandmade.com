Payments and vendors

When are wedding vendor payments due? A schedule by vendor

Every vendor sets its own deposit, its own installments and its own final due date. Here is the typical pattern, the month it all lands, and a simple way to never pay twice or pay late.

Updated October 24, 20268 minute readSources listed at the end

In short

  • Most vendors take a deposit of about 20 to 50 percent when you book. It is usually non-refundable.
  • Most final balances are due between about one month and two weeks before the wedding. Some venues ask earlier; some vendors are paid on the day or after.
  • Because of that, the last month is usually the most expensive month of the whole plan.
  • Track one row per payment, not one row per vendor, with a due date, who paid, and how.

The usual pattern: deposit, maybe an installment, final balance

Couples hire about 13 vendors on average, according to The Knot's 2026 study. Almost every one of them follows some version of the same three steps:

  1. Deposit at booking. It holds your date. Often 20 to 50 percent of the contract, and often non-refundable.
  2. An installment for bigger contracts, especially venues and caterers.
  3. The final balance, usually a few weeks before the wedding, based on final details like headcount and hours.

Couples on Reddit describe the same shape in their own contracts. One couple wrote that their venue took a 40 percent deposit with the rest due two months before, and their photographer took 35 percent up front. Another thread collected about twenty payment schedules from different couples, and no two were the same.

A payment plan with a vendor usually means paying in pieces by the original due date, not moving the due date to after the wedding. That is how one planner interviewed by The Knot put it, and it is worth asking about before you sign.

Why the last month gets expensive

Deposits are spread out over the year, because you book vendors one at a time. Final balances are not. Most of them land in the same few weeks before the date. Couples keep posting about it: the photographer, the venue and the florist all due the same week, right when the guest count and seating are being finalized too.

The fix is not more willpower. It is seeing the final month coming. Six to eight weeks out, list every payment still due, the method each vendor accepts (some only take a check or a bank transfer), and the total. Then compare it with what is actually set aside.

What goes wrong, from real planning threads

We read 109 wedding planning threads before building our workbook. Payments came up more than any other problem. The same few mistakes repeat:

  • "I thought you paid it." Partners each assume the other paid a deposit, and it turns out nobody did.
  • The deposit falls out of the total. One couple found their sheet was off by about $4,000 because deposits were noted but never added up.
  • No proof of payment. One couple's caterer said a $5,000 payment had never arrived. A receipt and the payment date settled it.
  • Formula slips. A homemade sheet forgets to sum a few rows, and the "real" number appears late.

Treat each payment as its own line, with a date, an amount, who paid and how. That one habit prevents almost every problem above.Morrow & Made

How to track wedding vendor payments

  1. One row per payment, not per vendor. A photographer with a deposit, an installment and a final balance gets three rows.
  2. Copy the due dates from the contract the day you sign. Not from memory, not from the email thread.
  3. Leave "paid date" empty until it is paid. That way the sheet always knows what is still open.
  4. Record who paid and the method: you, your partner, family; card, transfer or check.
  5. Save the receipt next to the contract.
  6. Check the list monthly, then weekly in the last two months.

Questions

When are final payments due for wedding vendors?

Most vendors want the final balance between about one month and two weeks before the wedding. Caterers often bill after your final headcount, venues can ask earlier, and some vendors are paid on the day or after it. Your contract sets the real date.

How much is a typical wedding vendor deposit?

Commonly 20 to 50 percent of the contract at booking, and it is usually non-refundable. Check the contract for the exact amount and what happens if you cancel or move the date.

Should I pay vendors early?

Some couples pay early for peace of mind. If you do, get a receipt and confirm the amount is credited to your balance. Paying early does not usually change refund terms, so read those first.
