Guests and budget

How much does each wedding guest cost? The per-head math

The national average is $292 a guest. But take one name off the list and you will not save $292. Here is what each guest really adds, and how to check it against your own quotes.

The average cost per wedding guest

The Knot surveyed 10,474 US couples who married in 2025. Their average wedding cost about $34,000 for 117 guests, which works out to $292 per guest. That number has climbed from $214 in 2019.

It is a useful benchmark, and also the reason so many couples get surprised. Couples keep posting the same story in planning forums: they picked a guest count first, multiplied by a number they read online, and the real quotes came back higher. The average blends every city and every size of wedding. Half of 2025 weddings cost less than $24,000, the median, and the bigger weddings pull the average up.

How guest count moves the total

Guest count is the lever with the most reach. It sets the catering bill, the bar, the rentals, and often which venues are big enough. The Knot found that couples spending under $12,000 averaged 92 guests, while couples spending over $41,000 averaged 141.

This chart is the version most people do in their heads. It is fine for a first rough number. It is not fine for deciding whether to cut ten names, and here is why.

What one more guest really adds

The $292 average divides everything by the guest count, including costs that do not change when one more person comes: the venue rental fee, the photographer, the officiant, your attire, the DJ. Those are fixed. Cut a guest and they stay the same.

What changes is the per-head part:

  • Catering, usually priced per plate or per person. The Knot's 2025 average was $80 a head, with the middle half of couples paying roughly $60 to $130.
  • Drinks, if the bar is priced per person.
  • Cake and desserts, often priced per slice.
  • Rentals: chairs, linens and place settings.
  • Stationery, postage and favors.
  • Service charge and tax on all of the above.

The average tells you how expensive weddings are. The per-head number tells you what each name on your list costs.Morrow & Made

A worked example

Our Wedding Planning OS workbook comes with a filled, fictional example wedding so you can see how the numbers connect. In that example, the per-guest prices are catering $70, bar $24, and cake $6, so $100 a head before rentals and favors. Here is what cutting 20 guests saves, two ways:

Using the $292 average × 20 guests$5,840
Using the real per-head prices: $100 × 20 guests$2,000
Difference between what you hoped to save and what you save$3,840

Cutting 20 guests is still real money, and it may let you choose a smaller room. It is just not the $5,840 the average suggests. Knowing that before the guest list conversation makes it a calmer one.

How to find your own cost per guest

  1. List your per-head prices. From each quote, pull out anything priced per person: catering, bar, cake, rentals, favors.
  2. Add the service charge and tax your caterer and venue apply. These are easy to miss and can be large.
  3. Add it up. That total is what one more guest really costs you.
  4. Count households, not just people. Invitations and postage go per household.
  5. Set the guest count last. Once you know the per-head number, you can see what 10 more or 10 fewer names really means.

Not every vendor charges per person. Some venues include tables and chairs in a flat fee, and some caterers have a minimum spend. Read each quote for "per person", "minimum" and "service charge" before you do the math.

Questions

What is the average wedding cost per guest?

$292 per guest for US weddings in 2025, with 117 guests on average, according to The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study.

How much do I save by cutting one guest?

Usually much less than $292, because the average includes fixed costs. You save the per-head costs: catering, drinks, cake, rentals, stationery and favors, plus tax and service charge on them.

Is $100 a head a good number to use?

It is the per-head total in our fictional example, not a benchmark. Use your own quotes. Catering alone averaged $80 a head in 2025, so your real number may be higher.
